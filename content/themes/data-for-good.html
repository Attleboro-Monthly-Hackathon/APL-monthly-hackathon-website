Theme: Visualizing local info

Light experience

Take a CSV of public data and produce a small set of clear charts. Prefer one dataset and three focused questions over a sprawling dashboard.

Project ideas

  1. Transit schedules — Which routes run most often? Busiest hour of day? Weekend vs weekday.
  2. Park / recreation locations — Count by neighborhood or park type; simple bar or pie chart.
  3. Local weather history — Average high/low by month; rainy days per year; year-over-year comparison.
  4. Library circulation (if available) — Most checked-out genres, seasonal spikes, or branch comparisons.

Learning goals

  • Loading and cleaning CSV data
  • Basic aggregation (group by, counts, averages)
  • Choosing the right chart for the question

Heavy experience

Build an interactive map or dashboard that lets someone explore local data over space and/or time.

Project ideas

  1. Crime / safety map — Points or heatmaps over neighborhoods; filters by year or category (use official open data only; handle sensitivity carefully).
  2. Tree canopy / green space — Choropleth or markers showing coverage by area.
  3. Historical census — Population, age, or housing over decades on a map or animated timeline.
  4. Transit accessibility — Walk distance to nearest stop; coverage gaps.
  5. Air quality or weather stations — Live or historical readings plotted on a map.

Learning goals

  • Joining tabular data to geography (lat/lon or boundaries)
  • Interactive filters and tooltips
  • Deploying a small app others can open in a browser

Finding local data

  1. Search [your city] open data portal.
  2. Check state GIS / DOT sites for transit and parks.
  3. Fall back to national sources (Census, NOAA, Data.gov) filtered to your area.
  4. Document the source and license on your chart or README.

Suggested session flow

  1. Agree on one question the charts should answer.
  2. Download or fetch a small clean dataset (or a sample if APIs are slow).
  3. Plot first, polish labels and colors second.
  4. Share: one insight you didn’t expect from the data.
